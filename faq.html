Frequently Asked Questions

Find answers to common questions about Oral Lesion and our technology.

Our AI technology has been trained on thousands of verified images and achieves high accuracy rates in identifying potential oral lesions. However, it's important to note that OralVision is a screening tool and not a replacement for professional medical diagnosis. We always recommend following up with a healthcare provider for any concerning findings.

Our AI analysis provides results almost instantly, usually within seconds of uploading your image. However, in some cases, a more detailed review may be necessary, which could take a few minutes.

If our system identifies a potential concern, we'll provide clear next steps and recommend consulting with a healthcare provider. Early detection is crucial for successful treatment of oral cancer, so prompt follow-up is important. The results will include information about the finding and guidance for discussing it with your doctor.

Yes, OralVision can be used as a regular screening tool between dental visits. We recommend checking once every few months, or whenever you notice any changes in your oral cavity. Regular screening can help catch potential issues early.

OralVision uses advanced deep learning neural networks, specifically convolutional neural networks (CNNs), to analyze oral cavity images. Our AI models have been trained on thousands of verified medical images to detect patterns associated with various oral conditions, including pre-cancerous and cancerous lesions.

Our platform supports common image formats including JPEG, PNG, and HEIF/HEIC. For optimal results, we recommend using high-resolution images with good lighting.

Yes, OralVision is a web-based platform that works on all modern browsers including Chrome, Safari, Firefox, and Edge. You can access it from desktops, laptops, tablets, and smartphones.

OralVision has been trained to identify various oral conditions including leukoplakia, erythroplakia, lichen planus, oral submucous fibrosis, and potential squamous cell carcinomas. The system can also identify common benign conditions to help reduce false positives.

Oral cancer is the sixth most common cancer globally, with hundreds of thousands of new cases diagnosed each year. Risk factors include tobacco use, excessive alcohol consumption, HPV infection, and sun exposure to the lips.

Early signs of oral cancer can include persistent sores or ulcers that don't heal within two weeks, white or red patches in the mouth, unexplained bleeding, numbness or pain in any area of the mouth, lumps or thickening of the oral tissues, difficulty chewing or swallowing, and persistent sore throat or hoarseness.

Yes, we take data security and privacy extremely seriously. All images and health information are encrypted both in transit and at rest. We comply with global healthcare data regulations and never share your information with third parties without your explicit consent.

By default, images are processed for analysis and then deleted within 24 hours. You can opt to save your images in your secure account for tracking changes over time or for sharing with healthcare providers. You can delete saved images at any time.

We collect minimal personal information required to provide our service. Your data is never sold to third parties. We may use anonymized data to improve our AI models, but this never includes personally identifiable information. You can request a full copy or deletion of your data at any time.
